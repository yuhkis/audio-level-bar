# Audio Level Bar agent instructions

These product and safety boundaries hold for every change. Read a task reference below only when it applies.

## Product and safety boundaries

- Do not record, persist, or transmit audio samples. System-output capture and the processed microphone are explicit opt-ins: keep `NSAudioCaptureUsageDescription` in the bundle, and obtain the additional system-audio recording permission only when one of them is enabled.
- With system-output monitoring off, the menu-bar speaker value is the macOS output volume setting. With monitoring on, the colored meter and number are live system-playback RMS dBFS, and the white marker is the output volume setting.
- The menu-bar microphone value is live RMS dBFS from the current macOS default input, measured by a HAL IOProc bound to that one device. The app never opens a Bluetooth or Bluetooth LE microphone by itself, nor an aggregate device whose full composition (`kAudioAggregateDevicePropertyFullSubDeviceList`, active or not, nested aggregates followed) names a Bluetooth or Bluetooth LE member, or a member that cannot be resolved to a device and a read transport, or no member at all, either of which counts as Bluetooth, whether or not a panel is showing and whatever the processed microphone is doing. For such a default input the value is `--`, never a measured silence: opening it would put the headset into call mode and degrade its playback, and a headset member connecting recomposes the aggregate under the app's running IO.
- Label the adjustable microphone scalar as input gain, not microphone level. Show sliders only when Core Audio reports that the property is settable, and never infer 100% for a device that does not report a volume property.
- Never destroy an aggregate device while another process has it open, in the app or in a throwaway experiment: it stops coreaudiod answering any process on the machine, and SIP means the only way back is a restart. Read `kAudioDevicePropertyDeviceIsRunningSomewhere` immediately before the destroy, and treat a failed read, other than a bad object status, as in use.
- Never let the microphone meter, the processed microphone's capture, or its publisher follow the default device implicitly. The meter, the capture with processing off or WebRTC, and the publisher in every mode are IOProcs on an explicitly chosen `AudioDeviceID` whose transport is re-read immediately before it is opened, and again once its listeners, those on an aggregate's active and full sub-device lists included, are in place, immediately before `AudioDeviceStart` (`InputOpenSequence` for the inputs, `PublisherOutputPolicy` for the publisher's output); no `AVAudioEngine` or AUHAL there, and so no default-device aggregate (`CADefaultDeviceAggregate-<PID>`) made for them. A framework engine that follows the default moves itself onto an arriving Bluetooth headset, and its own restart while the headset reconfigures can leave coreaudiod waiting for every process; the publisher's `AVAudioEngine` did exactly that in the build 56 hardware gate although its output node was bound to the built-in output. The publisher's output is a built-in output, or else a default output whose transports were all read and none of them is Bluetooth (`PublisherOutputPolicy`), chosen from the output devices read uncached immediately before the start. With neither, the processed microphone refuses to start, as for a Bluetooth microphone, and running IOProcs are rebuilt once the poll shows the bound output gone, involving Bluetooth, or an aggregate device that is no longer the default output (its active sub-devices are then unread). A built-in output is not read again before the create and the start: it never becomes Bluetooth and never goes away. The refusal is latched only when the poll's own choice is not a built-in output: an uncached read that finds no built-in output there failed, and the window is armed instead. The publisher renders the ring into its output, resampled when the rates differ, and its gate, closed on the main thread before the muted tap is withdrawn, makes it render silence while its release still waits on the lifecycle queue. Apple voice processing is the one `AVAudioEngine` left, and it is experimental and hidden: it is offered only when the `experimentalAppleVoiceProcessingEnabled` default is set at launch, a saved choice of it otherwise reads as off, and the hardware gate does not cover it. When offered, it does not run while any Bluetooth or Bluetooth LE audio device is connected, default or not, nor while whether one is connected cannot be read, nor while the default input or output involves Bluetooth. Once voice processing is enabled, and before any format is read, its Voice Processing I/O unit is bound to the input and output the start checked (`kAudioOutputUnitProperty_CurrentDevice`, element 1 and element 0) and both are read back; a set that fails or a read-back that differs refuses the start.
- Hold every IOProc, process tap, and aggregate device create, destroy, start, and stop for the quiet window: 3 s after the last Bluetooth connect or disconnect or default-device change, and never more than 15 s after the first. A change noted before that cap extends the window; only one noted at or after the cap is carried into the next window, which opens once the release's evaluation has acted. A release waits for a poll issued at or after the window's end to have applied and, up to the cap, for the pickers' default-device writes to complete; no evaluation admits starts while such a write is outstanding, armed or not. A release forced by the cap within 3 s of the last change, or with a change carried past the cap, issues only stops, destroys, and owed teardowns; creates, starts, publication, and processing starts and restarts wait for a release 3 s after the last change. Property listeners and IOProcs never stop, destroy, or rebuild from their callback; they close their level gate and report to the owner. On the main thread, with no HAL work, a report arms the window and owes the rebuild to the evaluation at a release: the microphone meter's invalidation (`meter-invalidated`), a configuration change under Apple voice processing's engine, or a listener on the processed microphone's capture input or publisher output (`processing-invalidated`), the system playback meter's listeners (`output-invalidated`), its levels no longer arriving for 4 s as confirmed on a later main-queue turn (`output-stale`), and a rebuild that finds the default output moved (`output-moved`). The system playback meter's listeners also advance the window's generation on their own queue at every notification of the current build, and each notification after the first re-arms the window, so its rebuild waits for the last one. Rebuilds of the system playback meter are spaced by `SystemOutputRecovery`, which only an issued rebuild or a reset clears, and give up at its `maximumConsecutiveInvalidations`th invalidation in a row: the meter then shows failed with Retry, and nothing is issued for it, neither a rebuild nor a stop, until Retry, monitoring switched on, an output change, or a wake (`give-up`). From the system's will-sleep to its did-wake (`PowerStateLog`), its levels are not watched, so `output-stale` is never reported, its invalidations are neither counted nor spaced, and no rebuild of it is issued, whatever asks for one; the did-wake, with monitoring on, arms the window (`wake`), starts its count over, and owes it exactly one rebuild, due at the release; restarts of the microphone meter are spaced by `MeterSession`, which gives up at its `maximumStartAttempts`th failed start in a row on one input: each failed start has released what it created, so nothing is left open, the meter shows its error with Retry, and no start is issued for it until Retry, another input, a start of the processed microphone, or a coreaudiod restart (`give-up`). With the input unchanged, no timer, wake, device-list or default-device notification, or poll starts it again, and Retry only starts the count over and asks for an evaluation, which holds that start like any other. The processed microphone's capture retries an input that only its own open-time read refused, for a transport it could not read or an aggregate member it could not resolve although the poll had, with the meter's spacing and cap behind a `processing-refused` window, never latching it as a Bluetooth refusal, and ends failed with Retry (`CaptureInputRetry`, `give-up`); a refusal the poll's own decision makes stays a refusal. A stops-only release leaves a system playback meter that is owed a rebuild running. Listeners on the HAL system object for the default devices and a coreaudiod restart also arm the window, advancing its generation on their own queue first (`quiet-window advance`); a device-list notification only asks for a poll, and one dropped behind a poll in flight is issued when that poll returns. A poll in flight for more than 0.5 s arms the window once (`poll-slow`). An evaluation that reads a generation the main thread has not armed the window for holds and arms it (`generation-advanced`), and the starts and stops an evaluation issues carry the generation the main thread has seen. After a coreaudiod restart nothing the app held is stopped or destroyed: each owner drops its IDs without a HAL call (`forget`), the meters and the publication already on the listener queue, and the evaluation after the window rebuilds. A start or stop already on its way compares the window's generation immediately before each HAL call and abandons that call if a window armed after it was issued; whatever it would have released is released after the window. The stop of Apple voice processing's engine, made on the main thread, compares it too: a stop whose generation is no longer current does not stop the `AVAudioEngine` (`interrupt-stop`), the engine counts as running with a restart owed, and the publication that stop was to precede is not withdrawn under it; the release after the window stops it. The processed microphone's IOProcs, the capture's and the publisher's, are released on their lifecycle queue, capture first, with the publisher's gate closed on the main thread before the release is queued, so the publication may be withdrawn while that release waits or is held back. A capture start whose publisher fails after a window armed is not failed either: its unstarted IOProc is kept (`interrupt`), the processing mode stays, and the restart after the window releases it. The processed microphone's publication, whose calls run one after another on the main thread (the tap's and the aggregate device's create, and the permission probe IOProc's create, start, stop, and destroy), takes the generation it was issued in once and compares it the same way: a listener's advance between two of its calls ends it before the next one (`interrupt`, with `reason=before-<call>`), and inside the window it issues nothing more, neither a create or start nor a stop, destroy, or rollback, a failed publication's rollback included. What it created is kept, never reported as published and with no in-use listener, and a probe the advance found started keeps running, as a meter does while the window holds. The first release, stops-only included, rolls it back rather than completing it, through the withdrawal's own path and in-use rule (the probe's stop and destroy, the aggregate device unless a process has it open, then the tap), each call compared against that release's generation (`interrupt-stop`), and a release that admits starts then publishes again if that is still wanted and allowed. The third publication in a row that a window interrupts, with none completed between them, is rolled back like the others and not issued again, so a public aggregate device is not created and destroyed at every window: the processed microphone then shows failed with Retry (`give-up`, `PublicationInterruptions`), and Retry, switching it off, or a publication that completes starts the count over. A release makes its calls one at a time in `LifecycleTeardown`'s order (stop, IOProc, aggregate device, tap) and drops each handle only after its own call has returned, so an interrupted release never destroys an aggregate device while its IOProc exists, nor a tap while its aggregate device exists. After an `AudioDeviceStart` that did not return `noErr`, the release reads `kAudioDevicePropertyDeviceIsRunning` first and stops IO that reports running before destroying its IOProc (`reason=failed-start`, which exempts nothing). Launch arms the window before anything else (`launch`), so nothing the app builds at launch is created or started before a release. The cleanup of what an earlier run left behind waits for the first release that admits starts, runs before anything else that release issues, and holds starts until it has run (`LaunchCleanup`). It removes the published device unless a process has it open, and only then destroys orphaned process taps: never one an existing aggregate device lists, no published-microphone tap while an earlier publication exists, none while an aggregate device of the app is running (`TapReclaimPlan`), and none when the device list or a listed device's transport cannot be read. When it has destroyed the earlier run's published device, which may have been the default input, that evaluation issues nothing more and re-arms the window (`launch-cleanup`). When the published device is in use, its tap stays and the reclaim waits for the owed teardown to complete. Its destroy lines carry `reason=launch`, which exempts nothing: the cleanup's destroys, like those of the publication's owed teardown, carry the generation their evaluation saw and are compared immediately before each call, and one a window holds back stays owed for the release after it. Exactly three operations are not held: Quit (`quit`), which, when it arrives while the window holds, removes the menu bar item at once and defers its teardown until 3 s after the last change the window noted, a carried one included, reading the window again each time the deferral comes due and never waiting past 3 s after the request (`quit-deferred`, written for each wait), and meanwhile polls, evaluates and issues nothing; the teardown then closes the level gates, the publisher's among them, stops Apple voice processing's engine if one runs and withdraws the publication synchronously on the main thread, and stops the meters and the processed microphone's IOProcs on their lifecycle queues at `.userInteractive`, waiting for them while some queue has finished a HAL call within the last 3 s, never more than 8 s in all, and leaving what an unfinished stop holds to coreaudiod's cleanup of the exiting client (`completed=false` names each such queue and its phase; the bound covers only the queued stops and counts from the start of the wait, so neither the deferral nor the main-thread engine stop and publication withdrawal before it count toward it, and no window armed before or during the teardown extends, shortens or interrupts it); as a last resort, the immediate stop and destroy of an IOProc whose open aggregate device gains an active Bluetooth sub-device (`bluetooth-sub-device`), which can only follow an edit of the aggregate while it is open, since none that names a Bluetooth member is opened, and which never starts anything; and the stop of Apple voice processing's engine, synchronously on the main thread, before the app's own picker writes a default device that is not known to be free of Bluetooth (`bluetooth-selection`), issued before that change begins, starting nothing, and releasing no IOProc: the publisher's gate is closed and its release owed to the evaluation after the window, and only while no window is armed or carried, no generation is unseen, no default write is outstanding, no restart is owed, and no Bluetooth device is connected or unread; otherwise the picker refuses such a pick and issues neither the stop nor the write (`picker refuse`). Their log lines carry the `reason=` in parentheses. The hardware gate judges its quiet-window criterion by `gen=`, and exempts from it only `bluetooth-selection` lines and the `quit` lines that come once the last logged `quit-deferred` wait has ended or, with no `quit-deferred` line, once no change the window noted is less than 3 s old; a `quit` line that should have been deferred fails. It judges every stop and destroy, whatever its reason, against the Bluetooth configuration-change requests. Some Core Audio calls still run synchronously on the main thread rather than on a lifecycle queue: the processed microphone's publication, the rollback of one a window interrupted, its checks, and its teardown (at Quit too) and its in-use listener; the prepare, start, and stop of Apple voice processing's `AVAudioEngine`, and the bind of its unit to its input and output with the read-back of both elements, including its stop at Quit, which runs before the bounded wait; the read-back of the unit's bound devices on its log lines and in the configuration-change observer, which never reads a moved device's transport; the uncached default-device ID reads immediately before and after the Apple voice-processing engine starts; the device-list, default-device, and output-UID reads; the control reads at launch; and the launch cleanup's removal of an earlier run's published device and its orphaned-tap reclaim. They are the known exceptions listed in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md#メインスレッドに残る同期的なhal処理).
- Write a `hal-lifecycle` `<operation>-begin` line immediately before every IOProc, process tap, and aggregate device create, start, stop, and destroy, and every Apple voice-processing `AVAudioEngine` prepare, start, and stop, after the call's generation check has passed, and its result line with the status once the call returns (`HALLifecycleLog.begin` and `record`). Both lines carry `gen=`: the quiet window's generation the work was issued in, read once and carried on both lines, or `-` for the three operations the window does not hold and for the `--reclaim-taps` command (`reason=reclaim-command`), which runs outside the app and its quiet window. A line that belongs to no issued call carries the generation current when it is written. The lines of the process taps and aggregate devices the app creates carry their app-generated UIDs (`aggregate-uid=`, `tap-uid=`). Log every field public, and never a device name or the UID of a device the app did not create.
- Never force quit while the processed microphone is published; that is one of the ways to leave Core Audio wedged for the whole machine. Quit through the app's own Quit item or `osascript -e 'tell application id "io.github.yuhkis.audio-level-bar" to quit'`.
- At the start and at the end of a change, check the branch, the index, the changed paths, and the upstream state that matters. Preserve unrelated work, and stage only this task's paths.

## Completion

- Update `README.md` and `README.ja.md`, or `DESIGN.md`, in the same change whenever adding or changing a command, preference, keyboard shortcut, permission, or user-visible behavior. The two READMEs carry the same content in English and Japanese; changing one without the other leaves the pair inconsistent. `DESIGN.md` and `ROADMAP.md` are Japanese-only by decision.
- Run the checks that cover the change and report their results. Documentation-only work needs a diff and a check of the links it carries, not audio-device tests or a candidate build; see [verification scope](docs/development.md#verification-scope) for code and app changes.
- Distinguish the source, the built candidate, the installed app, and real-device verification. A passing unit test or a moving meter does not establish GUI, permission, or consuming-app audio behavior. Say explicitly what was not verified.
